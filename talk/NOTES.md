# The talk picture: notes on a rough sketch

A sketch to react to, with one polish pass. Checked in headless Chromium; open `talk/index.html` from disk.
Screenshots for each guide step are in `shots/`. No console errors, no requests beyond the three page files.

## What works
- Two fields, side by side (stacked under 760 px), built from the same seed at the same size: same dots, places and hues.
- Each field has its own Tight to Loose slider and behaves like the unit picture after its second pass.
- Touch the first field: light spreads there. One spark carries only the touched dot across the gap and lights the second field by its own packing.
- After both have lit: dots lit on both sides hold steady; dots lit only in the second field pulse; dots lit only in the first show as faint rings in the second.
- More touches add up on both sides. Moving either slider clears everything, so each setting starts clean.
- The guide runs through all five steps to the end line.

## Decided alone
- The first mind starts in the middle and the second a little toward Tight. If both started in the same place they would light identically and step 2 would show nothing.
- Sparks only go from the first mind to the second. "Swap the sliders" is done with the sliders, as guide 5 words it.
- There is no Skip and no Touch button, because the draft text has no words for them. Keyboard: focus the first field and press Enter or Space.
- A dot counts as lit when its share of the light is over about an eighth.
- In a closed tile, the marked dots show through the tile so steady and pulsing can still be told apart.
- With reduced motion there is no travelling spark and no pulsing; heard-only dots hold at a lower level instead.
- `talk/` copies its field code from `unit/` and shares no files with it. Looking inside a group is left out here.

## Lines that did not match the screen
- "A whole group was meant. One detail arrived." With the second mind at Loose, the one detail spreads thinly across much of the field, so it does not look like one detail arriving.
- "Each word brings up another whole group." Only when the words land in different groups; a second word in the same group brings up nothing new.
- "A whole group came up. Some of it was never meant." When what was meant already covers most of that group, very little pulses.

## What felt unclear even to me
- Three states at once (steady, pulsing, ring) is a lot to read without a legend, and the draft text never says what a ring is.
- Pulsing reads as "important" more than as "never meant".
- The first mind's side never shows what the second one got, so the gap is only visible on one side.
